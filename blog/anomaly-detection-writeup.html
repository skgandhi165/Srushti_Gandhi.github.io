What actually counts as an anomaly? Lessons from building a detection model

Replace this paragraph and the sections below with the real writeup for your anomaly detection project. This is a template showing the structure and tone to write in — direct, specific, and focused on what you actually found rather than a tour of your code.

The problem

Start with the actual situation: what process generated this data, what could go wrong, and why catching it mattered. Be concrete — numbers, frequency, cost of missing something.

Why the obvious approach didn't work

Most writeups skip this and go straight to the winning method. Don't. Describe what you tried first (e.g. a fixed threshold), why it looked reasonable, and specifically how it failed — too many false positives, missed a real event, whatever it was. This is the part that actually shows analytical thinking.

Example: a static threshold flagged 40% of normal daily variation as "anomalous," which made the alerts useless within a week.

What worked

Explain the method you landed on in plain language before naming the technique. What signal were you actually looking for? Then name the model/approach and why it fit that signal better than the alternative.

Result

State the outcome plainly, with the number: ~$1M in potential losses prevented. Then one sentence on how you'd know that number is real — how it was measured or validated.

What I'd do differently

One or two honest notes on limitations or what you'd try next. This is what separates a portfolio piece from a report — it shows you can evaluate your own work.
